# Content Creation

Guidelines and frameworks for creating effective marketing content across channels.

## Before Writing

- Use the company context Brezel provides (company profile / "Ihre Firma"): products, target customers, positioning, tone of voice, formal or informal address. Ask the user for missing facts — never invent customer names, figures, quotes or awards.
- Use records in this Brezel system (find them with the entity tools) for real proof points, e.g. reference projects or customer feedback, and check that quotes and customer names may be published.
- Clarify the language and form of address. German B2B content usually uses "Sie" unless the brand deliberately uses "du".

## Content Type Templates

### Blog Post Structure
1. **Headline** — clear, benefit-driven, includes the primary keyword (aim for 60 characters or less for SEO)
2. **Introduction** (100-150 words) — hook with a question, statistic, bold claim, or relatable scenario. State what the post covers. Include the primary keyword.
3. **Body sections** (3-5) — each with a descriptive H2; H3 for subsections. One core idea per section with evidence, examples, or data.
4. **Conclusion** (75-100 words) — summarize takeaways, reinforce the main message, include a call to action.
5. **Meta description** — under 160 characters, includes the primary keyword, compels the click.

### Social Media Post Structure
- **Hook** — first line grabs attention (question, bold statement, number)
- **Body** — 2-4 concise points or a short narrative
- **CTA** — what the reader should do next
- **Hashtags** — 3-5 relevant hashtags (platform-dependent)

### Email Newsletter Structure
- **Subject line** — under 50 characters, creates curiosity or states clear value
- **Preview text** — complements the subject line, does not repeat it
- **Header/hero** — visual anchor and one-line value statement
- **Body sections** — 2-3 scannable content blocks, each with a bold intro sentence
- **Primary CTA** — one clear action per email
- **Footer** — unsubscribe link, provider information (Impressum), social links

Newsletters require recipients' prior consent (typically double opt-in) under UWG §7 and GDPR/DSGVO. If it is unclear whether the recipient list has consent, flag it to the user.

### Landing Page Structure
- **Headline** — primary benefit in under 10 words
- **Subheadline** — supporting context
- **Hero section** — headline, subheadline, primary CTA, supporting image or video
- **Value propositions** — 3-4 benefit-driven sections
- **Social proof** — testimonials, logos, figures, reference snippets (only with permission)
- **Objection handling** — FAQ or trust signals (certifications, data protection, made in/hosted in Germany if true)
- **Final CTA** — repeat the primary call to action
- Links to Impressum and privacy policy; forms collect only the data needed

### Press Release Structure
- **Headline** — factual, newsworthy, under 80 characters
- **Subheadline** — optional context
- **Dateline** — city, date
- **Lead paragraph** — who, what, when, where, why in 2-3 sentences
- **Body paragraphs** — supporting details, quotes, context
- **Boilerplate** — standard company description
- **Press contact** — name, email, phone

### Case Study Structure
- **Title** — "[Customer] achieves [result] with [product/service]"
- **Snapshot** — customer, industry, company size, product used, key result
- **Challenge** — the problem the customer faced
- **Solution** — what was implemented and how
- **Results** — quantified outcomes
- **Quote** — customer testimonial (approved by the customer)
- **CTA** — learn more, request a consultation, read more references

## Writing Best Practices by Channel

### Blog
- Write plainly for broad audiences; adjust up for expert audiences
- Short paragraphs (2-4 sentences); subheadings every 200-300 words
- Bullet points and numbered lists to break up text
- At least one data point, example, or quote per section
- Active voice; front-load key information
- In German, avoid long noun chains and overly bureaucratic style

### Social Media
- **LinkedIn**: professional but human, paragraph breaks, personal stories and lessons perform well; ~1,300 characters before "see more". The most important B2B platform in DACH alongside XING.
- **Instagram**: visual-first captions, storytelling hooks, line breaks, hashtags at the end
- **Facebook**: conversational tone, questions drive comments, short posts for links
- **X**: concise and punchy, strong opening words, threads for longer narratives

### Email
- Subject lines that create urgency, curiosity, or state clear value
- Personalize where possible and permitted
- One primary CTA per email, visually distinct
- Scannable body: bold key phrases, short paragraphs, bullet points
- Test subject lines, send times, CTA copy, layout
- Mobile-first: most email is read on mobile

### Web (Landing Pages, Product Pages)
- Lead with benefits, not features
- Speak to the reader directly (in the agreed form of address)
- Minimize jargon unless the audience expects it
- Every section answers "so what?" from the reader's perspective
- Reduce friction: fewer form fields, clear next steps, trust signals near CTAs

## SEO Fundamentals

### Keyword Strategy
- One primary keyword and 2-3 secondary keywords per piece
- Primary keyword in: headline, first paragraph, one subheading, meta description, URL slug
- Secondary keywords naturally in body copy and subheadings
- Do not keyword-stuff — write for humans first

### On-Page SEO Checklist
- Title tag: under 60 characters, includes primary keyword
- Meta description: under 160 characters, includes primary keyword
- URL slug: short, descriptive, includes primary keyword (transliterate umlauts: ä → ae)
- H1: one per page, matches or reflects the title tag
- H2/H3: descriptive, secondary keywords where natural
- Image alt text: descriptive, keyword where relevant
- Internal links: 2-3 to related content
- External links: 1-2 to authoritative sources

### Content-SEO Integration
- Cover the topic comprehensively
- Answer related questions people search for
- Refresh high-performing content regularly
- Structure for featured snippets: definition paragraphs, numbered lists, tables

## Headline and Hook Formulas

### Headlines
- **How to [achieve result] [without common obstacle]** — "How to Answer Customer Enquiries Faster Without Hiring"
- **[Number] [adjective] ways to [achieve result]** — "7 Proven Ways to Reduce Customer Churn"
- **Why [common belief] is wrong (and what to do instead)**
- **The [adjective] guide to [topic]** — "The Complete Guide to Digital Invoicing"
- **[Do this], not [that]** — "Build a Community, Not Just an Audience"
- **What [impressive result] taught us about [topic]**
- **[Topic]: what [audience] needs to know in [year]**

### Hooks (Opening Lines)
- **Surprising statistic** (only with a real, citable source)
- **Contrarian statement**: "The best campaigns start with saying no to most channels."
- **Question**: "When did a marketing email last change what you bought?"
- **Scenario**: "Imagine knowing which message will land before the campaign goes live."
- **Bold claim** (must be defensible — misleading claims are also a UWG risk)
- **Story opening**: "Last quarter our team spent 20 hours a week on reporting. Here is what we changed."

## Call-to-Action Best Practices

### Principles
- Action verbs: "Get", "Start", "Download", "Join", "Try", "See"
- Be specific about what happens next: "Request your free consultation" beats "Submit"
- Create urgency only when genuine
- Reduce risk: "No obligation", "Cancel anytime", "Free for 14 days" (only if true)
- One primary CTA per page or email

### Examples by Context
- **Blog post**: "Read our complete guide to [topic]" / "Subscribe to our newsletter"
- **Landing page**: "Request a demo" / "Start free trial" / "See pricing"
- **Email**: "Read the full story" / "Save your seat" / "Reply and tell us"
- **Social media**: "Comment if you agree" / "Save this for later"
- **Case study**: "See how [product] can work for your team" / "Talk to us"

### Placement
- Above the fold on landing pages
- After establishing value in emails (not in the first sentence)
- At the end of blog posts
- In-line within content when contextually relevant
- Repeat the primary CTA at the bottom of long pages

## Review Before Delivery

- Facts, figures and quotes are verified or marked as placeholders for the user to fill in
- Form of address and tone match the brand
- Legal basics are covered for the channel (Impressum, consent, no misleading claims)
- Ask the user before anything is published or sent
