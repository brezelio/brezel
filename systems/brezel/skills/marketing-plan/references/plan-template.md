# Plan Template — The 13-Section Structure

The canonical template for every marketing plan generated by this skill. Each section has a purpose, a structure, and inline prompts for what to draft.

---

## Title block

```markdown
# {Company} — Marketing Plan v1

**Prepared by:** {Author}
**For:** {Owners / management team}
**Date:** YYYY-MM-DD
**Status:** Draft v1 — for team review
```

---

## Section 1 — Executive summary

**Purpose:** Lift-and-share. Management should be able to paste this into a board, bank or partner update without editing.

**Length:** 400–700 words. Tight.

**Structure:**
1. **One-sentence frame.** What does this plan optimize for? Not "more revenue" — something specific to this company at this stage.
2. **Three big bets, ranked by leverage.** Each is a paragraph. Bet = a high-conviction thesis about where the team should focus money and attention.
3. **What twelve months looks like, plausibly.** Bullet list. The plausible outcome state at the end of the plan horizon.
4. **90-day priorities.** Numbered list. The six (give or take) moves that ship in the first quarter.

**Voice notes:**
- Match the company's voice
- Direct, management-readable, no marketing-speak
- Use names and numbers (specific channels, specific metrics) — not abstractions
- Name tradeoffs explicitly when they matter

---

## Section 2 — Strategic frame

**Purpose:** Distill positioning, target customers, business-model logic and brand voice into a single page that any team member or new hire can read to orient.

**Length:** 800–1500 words.

### What {Company} is, in one sentence
Pulled from the company profile, website or the owners' own words.

### The category we're claiming
Is the company creating a new category, redefining an existing one, or competing in a defined category? Name it in 2–3 sentences.

### Who we're for (ideal customer profile, distilled)
Demographics / firmographics + stated problem vs. real problem + what they're actually buying. Tight, 4–6 bullets.

### The market-quality gate
Name the problem size × frequency quadrant; for "is the market big enough?" add the market definition, SOM range and attractiveness score (see `references/market-sizing.md`).

### The business model logic
How does the company make money? What is the customer-acquisition economics theory? Which channel compounds?

### Brand voice (the non-negotiable)
If the company has documented voice rules, list them. YES / NO vocabulary. CTA rules. Tone. Form of address (in German usually "Sie"). Every other section must respect these.

**Voice notes:**
- Don't invent positioning. Surface what's there.
- If positioning is unclear or contradicted across materials, flag it in Section 13's open decisions.

---

## Section 3 — Current state

**Purpose:** Anchor the plan in reality. What's the team, budget, in-flight work and stuck work *today*?

**Length:** 1000–2000 words.

### Team composition (marketing surface area)

| Person | Role | Marketing surface area |
|---|---|---|

Be honest about gaps. If there's no dedicated marketing person yet, name when one becomes necessary and what role — the first hire should be a strategist with two deep skills (e.g. product marketing + content), titled Manager or Lead, and execution can go to freelancers or small specialised agencies.

### Marketing budget (current)
- Paid advertising: €X/month
- Tools: list with estimated cost
- Agencies / freelancers: list
- Headcount: list
- Blended CAC: €X (must include salaries, content costs, tools, agencies — not just ad spend; see `references/budget-planning.md`)
- Current spend as % of revenue

State what the 90-day plan must produce *without* budget that isn't available yet.

### What's already done (acknowledge, then build on)

| Asset | Status | Marketing leverage |
|---|---|---|

Past campaigns, press, trade fairs, references, certifications, awards, notable customers. **Critical**: don't write a plan that ignores work the team is proud of.

### What's in-flight (drafted but not shipped)

| Item | Status | Blocker |
|---|---|---|

### What's stuck (and needs to unstick this quarter)

| Issue | Cost of inaction | Action |
|---|---|---|

### Audit rubric snapshot
Scored snapshot using `references/current-state-rubric.md`. If a prior scored audit exists, paste those scores in. Otherwise score from available materials and note "scored from materials" under the heading.

| # | Section | Score | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Positioning | 0–5 | |
| ... | ... | ... | ... |
| 17 | Internationalization | 0–5 / N/A | |

**Total: X / 85 (Y%)** (or the adjusted maximum when sections are N/A). Note the *shape* of strength and weakness — that shape is the gap the rest of the plan closes.

**Voice notes:** Honest > polished. If the metrics are bad, name them.

## Section 4 — Acquisition

**Purpose:** Answer "how do strangers become aware of us?" Map every channel: current state, planned moves, skipped (with reason).

**Length:** 1000–1800 words.

### Current state
Brief. What's working today, what's not, what the data shows about channel mix.

### The plan
Numbered "Moves." Each move is a paragraph (3–6 sentences) describing the channel, the thesis and the specific work. Common moves:

- **SEO and content** — keyword research, pillar/spoke structure, content cadence.
- **Local visibility** (for regional businesses) — Google Business Profile, reviews, regional directories, local press.
- **Owner-/founder-led channels** — LinkedIn for B2B, Instagram/Facebook for consumer and local businesses. Cadence, topics, owners.
- **PR amplification** — What's the credibility anchor? How to amplify it.
- **Events** — trade fairs, open days, webinars, conference talks.
- **Commerce surface** (if applicable) — online shop, marketplaces, retail partners.
- **B2B sales support** — case studies / references, partner pages, industry-specific content.
- **Partnerships** — associations, chambers, complementary businesses, resellers.
- **Paid layer (when budget allows)** — Google Ads, Meta, LinkedIn. Held until budget is confirmed.

### 90-day acquisition moves
Week-by-week breakdown of what ships in the first quarter.

### 12-month acquisition outlook
Quarter-by-quarter outcome state (Q1 / Q2 / Q3 / Q4).

### Execution
Who does it (in-house, freelancer, agency, AI employee in Brezel) and what tools or data are needed.

---

## Section 5 — Activation

**Purpose:** Answer "once someone tries us or contacts us, do they have an experience that converts?"

**Length:** 800–1500 words. Same structure as Acquisition (Current state / The plan / 90-day / 12-month / Execution).

**Common moves:**
- Bedrock fixes (broken contact forms, slow quote responses, broken signup or checkout)
- Lead response process (time to first reply, quote follow-up)
- Onboarding of new customers (welcome, first delivery, first use)
- Website / landing page conversion improvements

---

## Section 6 — Retention

**Purpose:** Answer "once someone becomes a customer, do they stay and buy again?"

**Length:** 800–1500 words. Same structure.

**Common moves:**
- Lifecycle email flows (welcome, post-purchase, service reminders, lapsed customer, win-back)
- Newsletter and preference handling (with consent documented — see the compliance note in SKILL.md)
- Service and maintenance contracts
- Customer service as marketing (moments that create stories and reviews)

---

## Section 7 — Referral

**Purpose:** Answer "do happy customers bring more customers, and at what cost?"

**Length:** 500–1200 words. Same structure.

**Common moves:**
- Referral or partner program (start with customers who already recommend you)
- Review requests at the moment of value
- Reference customers and case studies
- Gifting / vouchers (consumer)

---

## Section 8 — Revenue

**Purpose:** Answer "what do we charge, who pays, and how does it compound?"

**Length:** 500–1200 words. Same structure.

**Common moves:**
- Pricing audit (what's actually charged today vs. listed, discounts)
- Packaging and bundles, service add-ons, maintenance contracts
- Upsell / cross-sell to existing customers
- Sales material and quote templates

### Unit economics
Required table:

| Metric | Value | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Average revenue per customer (per month or year) | €X | From the records in this Brezel system or accounting exports |
| Blended CAC | €X | Includes all marketing costs, not just ads |
| Retention / repeat-purchase rate | X% | |
| Customer lifetime value (rough) | €X | |
| LTV / CAC | X | Health benchmark: > 3 |

If any are unknown, flag them in Section 13 as top open decisions.

---

## Section 9 — 90-day roadmap

**Purpose:** The execution layer. Every move ships within a named week, with an owner. Tables, not prose.

### Weeks 1–2 — Unblock
Highest-confidence, lowest-cost changes. Removing things that are broken.

| Move | Stage | Owner |
|---|---|---|

### Weeks 3–4 — Foundation
Foundational work. First content. First flows shipping. First tests live.

### Weeks 5–8 — Velocity
Compounding work begins. Content cadence. Repeat tests. Channel scaling.

### Weeks 9–12 — Compound
Second-order moves. Layered tactics. 90-day review prep.

---

## Section 10 — 12-month outlook

**Purpose:** Quarterly milestones with explicit budget assumptions, anchored to a defensible growth expectation.

### Framing
- **Budget method used.** Revenue-based or goal-based (see `references/budget-planning.md`). Show the math.
- **Annual budget total** + the experimental buffer (+10–20%).
- **Resulting end-of-year revenue goal.** Honest forecast, not a guarantee.
- **Growth pattern expected.** Most healthy growth is linear with occasional step changes (new segment, new channel, new offer). Don't promise exponential growth.

### Per quarter

#### Q{N} — Months {X}–{Y}
**Budget state:** what is available this quarter
**Focus:** one-sentence theme
**Outcomes by end of Q{N}:** 5–8 bullets
**KPI targets:** 3–5 numerical targets

Split effort roughly 70/20/10 across current channels, the next channel being built, and experiments.

## Section 11 — Resources and execution model

**Purpose:** Show how the plan gets executed with the team and budget that actually exist.

### Who executes what

| Function | Owned by (internal) | Executed by (in-house / freelancer / agency / AI employee) |
|---|---|---|
| Demand generation (growth) | | |
| Positioning and messaging (product marketing) | | |
| Content and trust building | | |

Strategy stays in-house; execution can be outsourced. If a strategic owner is missing, the first 90-day move is to name or hire one.

### Tools and data per AARRR stage

| Stage | Tools / data in use | Gap |
|---|---|---|

Include what the records in this Brezel system can provide (customers, orders, enquiries) and which recurring tasks AI employees in Brezel could take over.

### Budget allocation across stages and what changes when more budget becomes available

| Budget level | Headcount | Tools | Channels live |
|---|---|---|---|

## Section 12 — Tactical idea bank (optional)

**Purpose:** Show what is *possible* beyond what Sections 4–8 prescribe, and what is deliberately skipped.

| # | Idea | AARRR stage | Status | Note |
|---|---|---|---|---|

Status legend: **Now (Q1)** · **Q2** · **Q3+** · **Q4+ / long game** · **Skip** (with rationale: off-brand, not fundable, not relevant).

---

## Section 13 — Measurement, RACI, open decisions

### Measurement
**North star (proposed):** One metric that captures the business-model thesis, specific to the company (e.g. qualified enquiries per month for a project business, repeat-purchase revenue for a shop, LTV/CAC for a subscription business).

**Leading indicators by AARRR stage:**

| Stage | Leading indicators |
|---|---|

Every rate divides matching closed windows: count only cohorts whose observation window has ended and label the rest as pending.

**Review cadence:** weekly (who syncs on what), monthly (who reviews what), quarterly (plan recalibration).

### RACI

| Domain | Responsible | Accountable | Consulted | Informed |
|---|---|---|---|---|

### Open decisions blocking the plan
Ranked by impact. Each: name + impact + what's blocked.

### Appendix
Sources used: records, files provided by the user, web sources.

---

## Per-section heuristics for "is this section done?"

- **Section 1** — An outsider could understand the company's growth thesis from this alone.
- **Section 2** — Brand voice rules are explicit enough that any new copywriter could follow them.
- **Section 3** — All in-flight items have an owner and a blocker named.
- **Sections 4–8** — Each move names who executes it and what it needs.
- **Section 9** — Every row has an owner.
- **Section 10** — Each quarter states its budget assumption.
- **Section 12** — Skip list has rationale, not just absence.
- **Section 13** — North star is specific to this company (not generic "revenue growth").
